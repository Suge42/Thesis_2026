\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:context}

The global agricultural sector is currently facing unprecedented challenges.
Driven by rapid population growth, climate change, and the need for sustainable resource management,
modern agriculture must maximize crop yields while minimizing environmental impact \cite{fao_2017_future}.
Precision agriculture \cite{precision_farming_1, precision_farming_2} has emerged as a vital framework
to address these demands, relying on accurate, large-scale,
and timely information regarding land use and crop dynamics.
Traditionally, acquiring this data required manual field surveys and administrative declarations,
which are labor-intensive, costly, and difficult to scale across regional or national extents.

Over the past decade, Earth Observation (EO) satellite constellations have transformed agricultural monitoring.
In particular, the European Space Agency's Sentinel-2 mission provides open-access,
multispectral imagery at high spatial resolution (10--20\,m)
with a dense revisit interval of 5 days \cite{drusch_sentinel2_2012}.
By observing how spectral reflectance profiles evolve throughout the seasons \cite{tucker_1979},
satellite time series capture vegetation phenology---the cyclical stages of emergence,
peak vegetative vigor, senescence, and harvest.
This continuous stream of observations enables researchers to distinguish crop species based
on their characteristic temporal growth profiles rather than static spatial snapshots \cite{zeng_phenology_2020}.

Concurrently, advances in Deep Learning (DL) have established sequence modeling
as the standard paradigm for analyzing Satellite Image Time Series (SITS).
Early remote sensing approaches emphasized spatial feature extraction from multi-spectral patches.
However, empirical findings have demonstrated that the temporal dimension---the chronological
progression of spectral bands at the pixel level---carries
the primary discriminative signal for crop type identification.
Architectures ranging from Temporal Convolutional Neural Networks (1D-CNNs) \cite{cnn_1} and
Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) \cite{rnn_1} to self-attention mechanisms and
Transformers \cite{attention_is_all_you_need, garnot2020psetae} have shown strong
capabilities in modeling within-season crop dynamics.

Despite these achievements, operational multi-year crop mapping encounters three fundamental challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Irregular and Sparse Observations:}
        Optical satellite acquisitions are heavily obscured by persistent
        cloud cover, shadows, and atmospheric artifacts.
        This yields uneven temporal intervals that complicate regular time-series modeling.
    \item \textbf{Computational Bottlenecks:}
        Processing multi-temporal spatial grids across vast geographic scales induces severe
        GPU memory and throughput constraints, hindering hyperparameter optimization
        in High-Performance Computing (HPC) environments.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Myopia and Crop Rotation:}
        The vast majority of current models operate on an isolated single-year basis.
        By ignoring historical field trajectories, these models discard the agronomic
        constraints of crop rotation \cite{crop_rotation}---the deliberate sequence of alternating
        crops across consecutive years to maintain soil fertility and break pest cycles.
\end{enumerate}

To address these limitations, this thesis investigates a hierarchical,
multi-temporal crop mapping framework operating on dense 1D pixel-based spectral sequences.
This pixel-based formulation substantially reduces memory overhead,
allowing for extensive parametersearches under constrained compute resources. 

Architecturally, this work proposes a modular, two-tier learning design wrapped within a unified
PyTorch Lightning pipeline that explicitly decouples \textit{intra-year phenological extraction}
from \textit{inter-year transition modeling}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Intra-Year Backbones:}
        A comparative benchmark is established across different temporal sequence encoders tasked
        with extracting seasonal phenology from dense Sentinel-2 observations within individual years.
        We systematically evaluate classical self-attention via Transformer Encoders alongside
        emerging linear-time State Space Models (Mamba) \cite{mamba_ssm}, assessing their
        representational capacity and computational efficiency.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Year Sequence Heads:}
        The seasonal representations or emission logits produced across consecutive agricultural
        seasons are aggregated chronologically across multiple years (2017--2022).
        To model crop rotation dynamics, we examine a dedicated Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)
        inter-year head and evaluate it against a classical probabilistic counterpart:
        a Generalized Hidden Markov Model (HMM) informed by empirical agricultural occurrence matrices.
\end{itemize}

By decoupling intra-year feature representation from inter-year state transitions,
this study assesses whether explicitly learning multi-year rotation dynamics provides a
statistically significant improvement over standalone phenology models, establishing practical
guidelines for long-term satellite crop mapping.
