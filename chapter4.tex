\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{cha:conclusion}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This thesis addressed the technical challenges of operationalizing deep learning for large-scale agricultural mapping,
transitioning from single-year phenological analysis to multi-year crop rotation modeling.
By shifting the INVEKOS Sentinel-2 dataset to an optimized 1D parcel-aggregated pipeline,
the severe I/O constraints that typically hinder longitudinal SITS analysis were bypassed.
This engineering optimization enabled the deployment of an automated MLOps pipeline leveraging PyTorch Lightning to fairly evaluate all architectural variants.

The systematic evaluation answered the primary research questions with quantitative evidence.
Single-year baselines confirmed the limitations of analyzing growing seasons in isolation;
the Temporal 1D-CNN and standalone Discriminative Transformer achieved limited Macro F1-scores of 30.17\% and 33.18\%, respectively.
Furthermore, structural control tests explicitly motivated investigating the role of non-linear feature extractors:
when the neural backbones were replaced with linear projections, the HMM cascade's performance dropped to a 5.23\% Macro F1,
demonstrating that raw SITS data must be cleanly untangled before transition modeling can occur.

Ultimately, the multi-year architectures significantly improved classification performance by leveraging historical crop rotation context.
Both architectures successfully captured inter-year dependencies to improve over baseline models.
The established Generative Transformer-HMM achieved a robust Macro F1-score of 63.95\%.
Crucially, the novel Discriminative Transformer-LSTM reached an optimal Macro F1-score of 68.47\% and an Overall Accuracy of 82.07\%,
significantly outperforming both its single-year counterpart and the Bayesian cascade.
This result demonstrates that recurrent memory cells can intrinsically model inter-year temporal dependencies end-to-end,
potentially capturing complex agricultural cycles more effectively than empirically initialized probabilistic transition matrices. 
However, the contrasting performance drop of the Generative Transformer-LSTM (achieving only 40.12\% Macro F1)
indicates that the LSTM's success might be dependent on the discriminative formulation of its input features.
While we hypothesize that the LSTM requires standard biased logits to properly regulate its recurrent gates,
further targeted interpretability studies are required to confirm the precise mechanisms driving this behavior.


\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

While the optimized 1D pipeline and the Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM framework successfully mapped inter-year crop dynamics,
several direct limitations emerged from this study, guiding the direction of future research.

\subsection{Observed Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Loss of Spatial Context:}
        The necessary shift to a 1D spatial-mean pipeline to resolve hardware bottlenecks completely discarded field-level spatial textures,
        boundaries, and neighborhood planting contexts.
    \item \textbf{Restricted Temporal Population:}
        The sequence evaluation required filtering the dataset to include only fields with a consistent geometric presence across all six years.
        This explicitly excluded intermittent fields, shifting borders, and the inherent declarative noise of administrative registries.
    \item \textbf{Information Baselines:}
        The explicit temporal distances injected into the Transformer models via the AgroDays Positional Encoding created
        an uneven information baseline when compared to the implicit sequential ordering of the 1D-CNN.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{subsec:future_directions}

Building upon these findings and acknowledging the aforementioned constraints, several promising avenues remain for advancing agricultural remote sensing:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Integration of State Space Models (SSMs):}
        Time and computational constraints limited the backbones evaluated in this thesis to convolutions and self-attention.
        Recent advancements in State Space Models, particularly the Mamba architecture, offer the potential to process extraordinarily
        long temporal sequences with linear scaling rather than the quadratic complexity of Transformers.
        Evaluating a Mamba-LSTM or Mamba-HMM cascade could further reduce training times and memory overhead.
    
    \item \textbf{Reintroducing Spatial Context:}
        With the temporal sequence modeling now optimized, future work should explore lightweight methods to reintroduce spatial context.
        Utilizing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) or localized 2D super-pixels could allow the model to capture field boundaries and
        neighborhood planting patterns without incurring the massive computational cost of full 3D spatiotemporal convolutions.
    
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Data Fusion:}
        Optical Sentinel-2 time series are inherently irregular due to cloud cover, requiring interpolation or attention-based masking.
        Fusing these optical sequences with Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data from Sentinel-1 would provide weather-independent observations to help mitigate cloud occlusions, though this would not guarantee a completely uninterrupted observation stream.
        Future iterations of the Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM could leverage parallel cross-attention mechanisms to seamlessly integrate these modalities.
    
    \item \textbf{Large-Scale Domain Adaptation:}
        The multi-year sequence rules learned by the recurrent models were intrinsically tied to the climatic and agronomic practices of the Austrian INVEKOS dataset.
        Investigating transfer learning techniques to adapt these pre-trained crop rotation memories to entirely different geographical regions or climates represents a critical next step toward global precision agriculture.

    \item \textbf{Recurrent Interpretability and Agronomic Validation:}
        While the Bidirectional LSTM successfully improved multi-year mapping accuracy,
        further analytical studies are required to determine exactly \textit{what} the memory cells learned.
        Future work should implement targeted interpretability methods (such as analyzing the LSTM gating mechanisms
        or executing ablation studies on transition matrices) to verify whether the network extracts true agronomic rotation rules,
        or simply exploits label persistence and geographical redundancies.
\end{itemize}
