\section{Single-Year and Structural Baselines}
\label{sec:single_year_baselines}

To establish a rigorous benchmark for multi-year architectures, a comprehensive suite of single-year sequence models and structural controls was developed.
These networks are restricted to processing intra-year phenological data, providing a comparative standard to justify the complexity of multi-year modeling.

\subsection{Temporal 1D Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN)}
\label{subsec:cnn_baseline}

The first phenological baseline is a 1-Dimensional Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN).
By sliding convolutional filters across the time dimension of a pixel's spectral profile, the network efficiently extracts localized
temporal features and phenological shifts, a methodology that has proven highly effective for satellite time series classification \cite{pelletier_temporal_2019}.

The encoder consists of two sequential convolutional blocks.
The initial layer maps the 13 input satellite bands to 64 channels using a kernel size of 3 and a padding of 1 to preserve the temporal sequence length.
This is followed by a 1D Batch Normalization layer and an in-place ReLU activation to introduce non-linearity.
The second layer further projects the features from 64 to 128 channels, utilizing the same kernel size, padding, normalization, and activation parameters.

For classification, a point-wise convolution (kernel size of 1) is applied to map the extracted 128-dimensional features to the target crop classes.
Because the network produces predictions for each time step, a \textbf{masked} temporal mean pooling of
the unnormalized logits is executed across all time steps in the year to generate the final field-level classification,
explicitly ensuring that zero-padded tokens are excluded from the average.
It is important to note that with two convolutional layers utilizing a kernel size of 3, a stride of 1, and a padding of 1,
the local receptive field spans exactly five consecutive observations.
Because the input sequence is irregularly sampled due to cloud cover, these five observations do not correspond
to a fixed number of days, meaning the CNN relies strictly on sequence order rather than exact temporal distances.

\subsection{Standalone Transformer Encoders (Generative and Discriminative)}
\label{subsec:transformer_baseline}

To capture global temporal dependencies without being restricted to the narrow, sliding time windows of a CNN,
a standalone \textbf{Transformer Encoder} serves as the second primary baseline.
Utilizing Multi-Head Self-Attention \cite{attention_is_all_you_need}, this architecture evaluates the entire yearly sequence simultaneously,
mapping the complex growth stages of crops into dense representations \cite{russwurm_self_attention_2020}.

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/transformer_diagram.pdf}
    \caption{Architectural block diagram of the standalone Transformer Encoder (Generative and Discriminative variants).}
    \label{fig:transformer_architecture}
\end{figure}

Crucially, the output formulation of the Transformer is evaluated in two distinct variants to benchmark
their efficacy as both standalone classifiers and future emission models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Discriminative Variant:}
        Employs a standard classification layer to directly predict the posterior probability $P(c|x)$ of
        each crop class $c$ based on the input sequence representation $h$.
        This is formulated using a standard linear projection with bias, followed by a softmax function:
        $$ P(c|x) = \frac{\exp(w_c^T h + b_c)}{\sum_{k=1}^{K} \exp(w_k^T h + b_k)} $$
        where $w_c$ and $b_c$ are the weights and bias for class $c$, and $K$ is the total number of classes.

    \item \textbf{Generative Variant:}
        Utilizes a specifically modified, normalized output layer.
        This design treats all crop types as equally likely by default, which prevents the network
        from unfairly favoring the most common crops in the dataset \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
        It achieves this by removing the bias term $b_c$ and normalizing the weights, often incorporating a
        temperature scalar $\tau$ to sharpen the emission likelihoods:
        $$ P(c|x) \propto \frac{\exp(\frac{1}{\tau} w_c^T h)}{\sum_{k=1}^{K} \exp(\frac{1}{\tau} w_k^T h)} $$
        More importantly, generating these probabilities without prior bias matches the exact mathematical
        format required by the HMM to effectively map sequence transitions \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{AgroDays Positional Encoding}
\label{subsec:agrodays_encoding}

Agricultural satellite time series are inherently irregularly sampled due to cloud cover and varying satellite revisit times.
Standard sequential positional encodings would falsely inform a self-attention mechanism that all observations are equidistant.
To address this, all Transformer variants in this study (both the single-year baselines and the multi-year hybrid architectures)
utilize an AgroDays Positional Encoding module.

Instead of indexing observations as sequential integer steps, this module adapts the standard sinusoidal positional
encoding \cite{attention_is_all_you_need} by substituting the sequence index with the exact agricultural day of the acquisition.
This adaptation is a well-established technique in remote sensing time series analysis \cite{russwurm_self_attention_2020, garnot2020psetae}.
While the Euclidean distance between two sinusoidal encodings is a periodic function and therefore does not guarantee a strictly linear,
proportional preservation of temporal distance, this mapping effectively makes the actual acquisition dates explicitly available to the model.

\begin{algorithm}[htpb]
    \caption{AgroDays Positional Encoding (Lookup Table)}
    \label{alg:agrodays}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Maximum agricultural day $max\_seq \gets 366$
        \Require Pre-compute during initialization: 
        \State $table \gets \text{zeros}(max\_seq, d\_model)$
        \For{$pos = 0$ \textbf{to} $max\_seq - 1$}
            \For{$i = 0$ \textbf{to} $(d\_model/2) - 1$}
                \State $freq \gets pos / 10000^{2i/d\_model}$
                \State $table[pos, 2i] \gets \sin(freq)$
                \State $table[pos, 2i+1] \gets \cos(freq)$
            \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \State $PE\_Layer \gets \text{FrozenEmbedding}(table)$
        
        \vspace{1.5mm}
        \Require \textbf{Forward Pass:}
        \Require Input sequence $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times d\_model}$
        \Require Agricultural days tensor $pos \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T}$
        \Ensure Position-encoded sequence $X_{enc} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times d\_model}$
        \State $PE \gets PE\_Layer(pos)$ \Comment{Look up pre-computed vectors using day index}
        \State $X_{enc} \gets X + PE$ \Comment{Inject temporal signal into embeddings}
        \State \textbf{return} $X_{enc}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

As detailed in Algorithm \ref{alg:agrodays}, the position-aware embeddings are pre-computed during the network's initialization.
A geometric progression of wavelengths is calculated across the network's embedding dimension and stored within
a frozen lookup table \cite{attention_is_all_you_need}.
During the forward pass, the model simply uses the input matrix $pos$ as indices to extract the correct sine and cosine vectors
for each specific agricultural day, injecting this explicit temporal signal directly into the spectral embeddings.

To handle calendar variations such as leap years, the day index is bounded to a 366-day cycle.
For days lacking valid satellite acquisitions due to irregular revisit times,
no explicit temporal padding is required for the positional encoding itself;
instead, the overall sequence is zero-padded to a maximum length, and a standard padding mask is supplied
to the Transformer to ensure the attention mechanism ignores these artificial tokens.

\subsection{Linear Structural Controls}
\label{subsec:linear_controls}

To scientifically validate the necessity of the aforementioned non-linear backbones,
standalone multi-year controls utilizing a zero-memory linear baseline were evaluated.
Rather than relying on randomly initialized weights, this control relies on a fully trainable linear layer devoid of intermediate non-linearities.

Specifically, the raw 13-band SITS data is temporally mean-pooled across the growing season to completely
collapse the intra-year temporal sequence into a static feature vector.
A single trained linear layer then maps these 13 features directly to the 47 output classes.
This projection is jointly optimized alongside the sequence heads (LSTM and HMM) utilizing a standard Categorical Cross-Entropy loss.
Because a single linear layer applied to temporally pooled data cannot mathematically disentangle highly overlapping,
non-linear phenological profiles, it outputs near-random temporal features.
Furthermore, because this control utilizes a temporal mean, it entirely collapses the intra-annual temporal sequence.
Therefore, this configuration evaluates the dual impact of removing both non-linear feature extraction
and the chronological intra-year temporal structure.
Testing the sequence heads on these impoverished inputs isolates their transition-modeling capabilities,
explicitly evaluating whether multi-year sequence smoothing requires a high-capacity, non-linear feature extractor to function.


\section{Multi-Year Architectures}
\label{sec:multi_year_architectures}

The core focus of this thesis is the development and evaluation of hybrid, multi-scale neural architectures capable of simultaneously
modeling intra-year phenological dynamics and inter-year crop rotation patterns. 

\subsection{The Reference Transformer-HMM}
\label{subsec:transformer_hmm}

To evaluate purely neural multi-year models, an established state-of-the-art framework is required for comparison.
This research implements the Bayesian cascade architecture proposed by Perantoni et al. \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024},
which integrates a Transformer Encoder with a Hidden Markov Model (HMM).

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/transformer_hmm_diagram.pdf}
    \caption{Hierarchical overview of the Transformer-HMM approach, demonstrating the fusion of intra-year attention and inter-year transition modeling.}
    \label{fig:transformer_hmm_cascade}
\end{figure}

In this hierarchical setup, the Transformer acts exclusively as the emission model,
processing the intra-year SITS to estimate the emission likelihoods of each crop class for a given year.
These likelihoods are then passed into the HMM transition layer.
To ensure a scientifically rigorous comparison against the purely neural recurrent baseline,
it is crucial to detail the exact formulation of the HMM implemented in this thesis \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.

Rather than relying on frozen, manually defined agronomic rules, the HMM's explicit probabilistic
transition matrices are jointly optimized during training alongside the neural backbone \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
The transition model is formulated as a first-order, time-variant Markov chain;
this means a distinct transition matrix is utilized for each specific yearly interval
(e.g., $T_{2016 \rightarrow 2017}$ is independent of $T_{2017 \rightarrow 2018}$) to account for inter-annual
climate or policy shifts \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
To ensure realistic starting conditions, the matrices and initial prior distributions are empirically initialized
using the exact crop co-occurrence frequencies computed from the training dataset \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
To handle completely unobserved rotations and guarantee numerical stability, a smoothing constant ($1\times10^{-20}$)
is added to all transition counts before mapping them into log-space.
This prevents zero-probability transitions from causing catastrophic $-\infty$ log-likelihoods during training.
All subsequent computations are executed in log-space using the numerically stable log-sum-exp trick \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.

During training and inference, the architecture employs a forward-backward algorithm to perform marginal smoothing;
it computes the posterior probability of each crop class at year $t$ given the entire multi-year sequence of observations $x_{1:T}$.
The network's final output logits are derived by combining the forward and backward probabilities,
which mathematically translates to summing their log-probabilities (the log-space equivalent of probability multiplication),
to compute the smoothed marginals \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.

While Viterbi decoding (which seeks the single maximum probability sequence $\arg\max_{s_{1:T}} P(s_{1:T} | x_{1:T})$)
is also computed as a supplementary metric, the primary sequence optimization relies on the smoothed marginals.
Consequently, while the HMM heavily penalizes unlikely crop rotations, it does not enforce rigid, deterministic agronomic constraints;
rather, it balances the Transformer's emission likelihoods with maximum statistical temporal coherence.

\subsection{The Novel Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM}
\label{subsec:transformer_lstm}

As an alternative to Bayesian transition smoothing, this thesis proposes a novel, purely neural multi-year architecture:
the Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM. This model is designed to learn both intra-year phenology and inter-year crop rotations end-to-end,
without relying on fixed, explicit transition matrices.

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/transformer_lstm_diagram.pdf}
    \caption{Hierarchical overview of the Transformer-LSTM architecture, demonstrating the fusion of intra-year attention and inter-year recurrence.}
    \label{fig:hybrid_architecture}
\end{figure}

The architecture processes the temporal scales using a two-stage approach.
In the first stage, the sequence is flattened to merge the batch and year dimensions,
allowing the Intra-Year Transformer Encoder to evaluate all years independently and concurrently by flattening the temporal sequences into the batch dimension.
The Transformer's complex phenological outputs are temporally masked-mean-pooled
(strictly excluding artificial padding tokens) and passed through an intra-year classifier,
collapsing the dense $d\_model$ feature extraction into a sequence of real-valued scores (logits) for the 47 specific target classes.
Crucially, this intermediate 47-dimensional vector is not merely a latent representation;
the Intra-Year Transformer is explicitly optimized using an auxiliary intra-year classification loss during training.
This forces the intermediate outputs to represent calibrated semantic class predictions.
Furthermore, compressing the high-dimensional embeddings down to 47 semantic logits before recurrence drastically
reduces the LSTM's parameter footprint, forcing it to act strictly as an inter-year transition model rather than a redundant feature extractor.
While passing the full $d\_model$ embedding to the LSTM represents a valuable future ablation study,
this bottleneck isolates the evaluation strictly to class-level transition dynamics.

In the second stage, this chronological sequence of yearly predictions is restored and passed into an \textbf{Inter-Year Bidirectional LSTM} \cite{hochreiter_long_1997}. 

\begin{algorithm}[htpb]
    \caption{Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM Forward Pass}
    \label{alg:hybrid_forward}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Multi-year input tensor $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times Y \times C \times T}$
        \Require Agricultural days tensor $pos \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times Y \times T}$
        \Ensure Multi-year crop class logits $Y_{pred} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times n\_classes \times Y}$
        \vspace{1.5mm}
        \State \textbf{Stage 1: Intra-Year Processing (Transformer)}
        \State $X \gets \text{permute}(X, [0, 1, 3, 2])$ \Comment{Target shape: $[B, Y, T, C]$}
        \State $X_{flat} \gets \text{reshape}(X, [B \cdot Y, T, C])$ \Comment{Flatten for independent yearly processing}
        \State $pos_{flat} \gets \text{reshape}(pos, [B \cdot Y, T])$
        \State $X_{emb} \gets \text{LinearProjection}(X_{flat})$
        \State $X_{emb} \gets \text{AgroDaysPositionalEncoding}(X_{emb}, pos_{flat})$
        \State $T_{out} \gets \text{TransformerEncoder}(X_{emb})$ \Comment{Self-Attention over time steps}
        \State $Y_{emb} \gets \text{masked\_mean}(T_{out}, \text{dim}=1, \text{mask})$ \Comment{Masked temporal pooling, ignoring padding: $[B \cdot Y, d\_model]$}
        \State $Y_{logits} \gets \text{IntraYearClassifier}(Y_{emb})$ \Comment{Map to real-valued logits: $[B \cdot Y, n\_classes]$}
        \vspace{1.5mm}
        \State \textbf{Stage 2: Inter-Year Processing (LSTM)}
        \State $Y_{seq} \gets \text{reshape}(Y_{logits}, [B, Y, n\_classes])$ \Comment{Restore sequence of years}
        \State $L_{out}, (h_n, c_n) \gets \text{BidirectionalLSTM}(Y_{seq})$ \Comment{Model crop transitions}
        \State $L_{drop} \gets \text{Dropout}(L_{out})$ \Comment{Apply temporal regularization}
        \vspace{1.5mm}
        \State \textbf{Stage 3: Multi-Year Classification}
        \State $L_{logits} \gets \text{InterYearClassifier}(L_{drop})$ \Comment{Map LSTM hidden states to final classes}
        \State $Y_{pred} \gets \text{permute}(L_{logits}, [0, 2, 1])$ \Comment{Final shape: $[B, n\_classes, Y]$}
        \State \textbf{return} $Y_{pred}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The bidirectional nature of the LSTM is highly critical; it enables the network to analyze the rotation history in both the forward and backward temporal directions.
This allows the model to refine its current crop prediction based on deep inter-year transition rules learned from preceding and subsequent planting decisions.
Finally, the concatenated bidirectional hidden states are passed through a dropout regularization layer and a final linear classifier to output
the highly contextualized unnormalized logit scores for all target crop classes across the entire multi-year sequence.

The entire network is optimized end-to-end using a combined loss function:
$\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{inter} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{intra}$, where $\mathcal{L}_{inter}$ is
the primary cross-entropy loss from the multi-year sequence output, $\mathcal{L}_{intra}$ is the
auxiliary intra-year cross-entropy loss, and $\lambda$ is a weighting coefficient empirically
set to $1.0$ to balance feature extraction and transition modeling.
