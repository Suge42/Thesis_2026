\section{Single-Year and Structural Baselines}
\label{sec:single_year_baselines}

To establish a rigorous benchmark for multi-year architectures, a comprehensive suite of single-year sequence models and structural controls was developed.
These networks are restricted to processing intra-year phenological data, providing a comparative standard to justify the complexity of multi-year modeling.

\subsection{Temporal 1D Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN)}
\label{subsec:cnn_baseline}

The first phenological baseline is a 1-Dimensional Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN).
By sliding convolutional filters across the time dimension of a pixel's spectral profile, the network efficiently extracts localized
temporal features and phenological shifts, a methodology that has proven highly effective for satellite time series classification \cite{pelletier_temporal_2019}.

The \textbf{encoder} consists of two sequential convolutional blocks.
The initial layer maps the 13 input satellite bands to 64 channels using a kernel size of 3 and a padding of 1 to preserve the temporal sequence length.
This is followed by a 1D Batch Normalization layer and an in-place ReLU activation to introduce non-linearity.
The second layer further projects the features from 64 to 128 channels, utilizing the same kernel size, padding, normalization, and activation parameters.

For \textbf{classification}, a point-wise convolution (kernel size of 1) is applied to map the extracted 128-dimensional features to the 47 target crop classes.
Because the network produces predictions for each time step, a temporal majority vote is executed by averaging the unnormalized logit
scores across all time steps in the year to generate the final field-level classification.

\subsection{Standalone Transformer Encoders (Generative and Discriminative)}
\label{subsec:transformer_baseline}

To capture global temporal dependencies without being restricted to the narrow, sliding time windows of a CNN,
a standalone \textbf{Transformer Encoder} serves as the second primary baseline.
Utilizing Multi-Head Self-Attention \cite{attention_is_all_you_need}, this architecture evaluates the entire yearly sequence simultaneously,
mapping the complex growth stages of crops into dense representations \cite{russwurm_self_attention_2020}.

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/transformer_diagram.pdf}
    \caption{Architectural block diagram of the standalone Transformer Encoder (Generative and Discriminative variants).}
    \label{fig:transformer_architecture}
\end{figure}

Crucially, the output formulation of the Transformer is evaluated in two distinct variants to benchmark
their efficacy as both standalone classifiers and future emission models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Discriminative Variant:}
        Employs a standard classification layer to directly predict the probability of each crop class based on the input sequence.
    \item \textbf{Generative Variant:}
        Utilizes a specifically modified, normalized output layer.
        This design treats all crop types as equally likely by default, which prevents the network
        from unfairly favoring the most common crops in the dataset \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
        More importantly, it generates these probabilities in the exact mathematical format required
        by the HMM to effectively map sequence transitions \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Linear Structural Controls}
\label{subsec:linear_controls}

To scientifically validate the necessity of the aforementioned non-linear backbones, standalone multi-year controls utilizing a \textbf{simplistic linear backbone} were evaluated.
In these configurations, the raw 13-band spectral time series is projected through a single,
uninitialized linear layer ($W x + b$) directly into the sequence-modeling heads (LSTM and HMM).

These linear models serve as a deliberate \textbf{structural simplification}. Because a single linear layer cannot mathematically separate complex,
non-linear phenological profiles, it outputs near-random temporal features.
Testing the sequence heads on these impoverished features isolates the capabilities of the recurrent and probabilistic networks,
demonstrating that multi-year sequence smoothing fundamentally requires a high-capacity, non-linear feature extractor to function effectively.


\section{Multi-Year Architectures}
\label{sec:multi_year_architectures}

The core focus of this thesis is the development and evaluation of hybrid, multi-scale neural architectures capable of simultaneously
modeling intra-year phenological dynamics and inter-year crop rotation patterns. 

\subsection{AgroDays Positional Encoding}
\label{subsec:agrodays_encoding}

Agricultural satellite time series are inherently irregularly sampled due to cloud cover and varying satellite revisit times.
Standard sequential positional encodings would falsely inform a self-attention mechanism that all observations are equidistant.
To address this, the architecture utilizes a custom \textbf{AgroDays Positional Encoding} module.

Instead of indexing observations as sequential integer steps, this module adapts the standard sinusoidal positional
encoding \cite{attention_is_all_you_need} by scaling the frequencies using the exact agricultural day of the acquisition.
Mapping absolute day values onto continuous periodic functions ensures that the mathematical distance between any two
encoded time steps is strictly proportional to the actual temporal gap between them.

\begin{algorithm}[htpb]
    \caption{AgroDays Positional Encoding (Lookup Table)}
    \label{alg:agrodays}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Maximum agricultural day $max\_seq \gets 365$
        \Require Pre-compute during initialization: 
        \State $table \gets \text{zeros}(max\_seq, d\_model)$
        \For{$pos = 0$ \textbf{to} $max\_seq$}
            \For{$i = 0$ \textbf{to} $d\_model/2$}
                \State $freq \gets pos / 10000^{2i/d\_model}$
                \State $table[pos, 2i] \gets \sin(freq)$
                \State $table[pos, 2i+1] \gets \cos(freq)$
            \EndFor
        \EndFor
        \State $PE\_Layer \gets \text{FrozenEmbedding}(table)$
        
        \vspace{1.5mm}
        \Require \textbf{Forward Pass:}
        \Require Input sequence $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times d\_model}$
        \Require Agricultural days tensor $pos \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T}$
        \Ensure Position-encoded sequence $X_{enc} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times d\_model}$
        \State $PE \gets PE\_Layer(pos)$ \Comment{Look up pre-computed vectors using day index}
        \State $X_{enc} \gets X + PE$ \Comment{Inject temporal signal into embeddings}
        \State \textbf{return} $X_{enc}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

As detailed in Algorithm \ref{alg:agrodays}, the position-aware embeddings are pre-computed during the network's initialization.
A geometric progression of wavelengths is calculated across the network's embedding dimension and stored within
a frozen lookup table \cite{attention_is_all_you_need}.
During the forward pass, the model simply uses the input matrix $pos$ as indices to extract the correct sine and cosine vectors
for each specific agricultural day, injecting this explicit temporal signal directly into the spectral embeddings.

\subsection{The Reference Transformer-HMM Cascade}
\label{subsec:transformer_hmm}

To evaluate purely neural multi-year models, an established state-of-the-art framework is required for comparison.
This research implements the Bayesian cascade architecture proposed by Perantoni et al. \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024},
which integrates a Transformer Encoder with a \textbf{Hidden Markov Model} (HMM).

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/transformer_hmm_diagram.pdf}
    \caption{Hierarchical overview of the Transformer-HMM cascade, demonstrating the fusion of intra-year attention and inter-year transition modeling.}
    \label{fig:transformer_hmm_cascade}
\end{figure}

In this hierarchical setup, the Transformer acts exclusively as the \textbf{emission model},
processing the intra-year SITS to estimate the likelihood of each crop class for a given year.
The generated class probabilities are then passed into the HMM transition layer.
The HMM models the temporal correlation of the labels across the multi-year sequence utilizing explicit \textbf{transition
matrices}, probabilistic grids that mathematically quantify the likelihood of a farmer rotating from one specific crop type to another.
By performing cascade classification in both the forward and backward temporal directions, the HMM smooths the Transformer's emission predictions,
ensuring the final predicted sequence adheres to logical agronomic rotation practices \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.

\subsection{The Novel Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM}
\label{subsec:transformer_lstm}

As an alternative to Bayesian transition smoothing, this thesis proposes a novel, purely neural multi-year architecture:
the Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM. This model is designed to learn both intra-year phenology and inter-year crop rotations end-to-end,
without relying on fixed, explicit transition matrices.

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{images/transformer_lstm_diagram.pdf}
    \caption{Hierarchical overview of the Transformer-LSTM architecture, demonstrating the fusion of intra-year attention and inter-year recurrence.}
    \label{fig:hybrid_architecture}
\end{figure}

The architecture processes the temporal scales using a two-stage approach.
In the first stage, the sequence is flattened to merge the batch and year dimensions,
allowing the \textbf{Intra-Year Transformer Encoder} to evaluate all years independently and in parallel across the hardware.
The Transformer's complex phenological outputs are temporally mean-pooled and passed through an intra-year classifier,
collapsing the dense feature extraction into a sequence of unnormalized class probabilities (logits) for each specific year.

In the second stage, this chronological sequence of yearly predictions is restored and passed into an \textbf{Inter-Year Bidirectional LSTM} \cite{hochreiter_long_1997}. 

\begin{algorithm}[htpb]
    \caption{Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM Forward Pass}
    \label{alg:hybrid_forward}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Multi-year input tensor $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times Y \times C \times T}$
        \Require Agricultural days tensor $pos \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times Y \times T}$
        \Ensure Multi-year crop class logits $Y_{pred} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times n\_classes \times Y}$
        \vspace{1.5mm}
        \State \textbf{Stage 1: Intra-Year Processing (Transformer)}
        \State $X \gets \text{permute}(X, [0, 1, 3, 2])$ \Comment{Target shape: $[B, Y, T, C]$}
        \State $X_{flat} \gets \text{reshape}(X, [B \cdot Y, T, C])$ \Comment{Flatten for independent yearly processing}
        \State $pos_{flat} \gets \text{reshape}(pos, [B \cdot Y, T])$
        \State $X_{emb} \gets \text{LinearProjection}(X_{flat})$
        \State $X_{emb} \gets \text{AgroDaysPositionalEncoding}(X_{emb}, pos_{flat})$
        \State $T_{out} \gets \text{TransformerEncoder}(X_{emb})$ \Comment{Self-Attention over time steps}
        \State $Y_{emb} \gets \text{mean}(T_{out}, \text{dim}=1)$ \Comment{Temporal pooling: $[B \cdot Y, d\_model]$}
        \State $Y_{logits} \gets \text{IntraYearClassifier}(Y_{emb})$ \Comment{Map to logits: $[B \cdot Y, n\_classes]$}
        \vspace{1.5mm}
        \State \textbf{Stage 2: Inter-Year Processing (LSTM)}
        \State $Y_{seq} \gets \text{reshape}(Y_{logits}, [B, Y, n\_classes])$ \Comment{Restore sequence of years}
        \State $L_{out}, (h_n, c_n) \gets \text{BidirectionalLSTM}(Y_{seq})$ \Comment{Model crop rotations}
        \vspace{1.5mm}
        \State \textbf{Stage 3: Multi-Year Classification}
        \State $L_{logits} \gets \text{InterYearClassifier}(L_{out})$ \Comment{Map LSTM hidden states to target classes}
        \State $Y_{pred} \gets \text{permute}(L_{logits}, [0, 2, 1])$ \Comment{Final shape: $[B, n\_classes, Y]$}
        \State \textbf{return} $Y_{pred}$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The bidirectional nature of the LSTM is highly critical; it enables the network to analyze the rotation history in both the forward and backward temporal directions.
This allows the model to refine its current crop prediction based on deep inter-year transition rules learned from preceding and subsequent planting decisions.
Finally, the concatenated bidirectional hidden states are passed through a dropout regularization layer and a final linear classifier to output
the highly contextualized unnormalized logit scores for all target crop classes across the entire multi-year sequence.
