\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{cha:abstract}

Precision agriculture is increasingly vital for ensuring global food security and sustainable resource management,
heavily relying on the accurate, large-scale mapping of land use and crop distribution derived from Earth Observation satellites.
Specifically, the high-resolution, high-frequency multispectral imagery provided by the Sentinel-2 mission enables
the construction of continuous Satellite Image Time Series (SITS).
By monitoring how specific agricultural parcels reflect light over time, deep learning models can track the
unique phenological growth stages of varying vegetation to classify crop types.
Despite these advancements, employing sequence modeling for SITS on a national or
global scale presents severe technical and agronomic challenges.
Satellite imagery is inherently irregular due to persistent cloud cover, atmospheric interference,
and varying sensor revisit times, resulting in noisy sequences with unequal temporal gaps.
Furthermore, processing high-dimensional spatial-temporal grids creates critical memory and
input/output (I/O) bottlenecks in High-Performance Computing (HPC) environments,
severely limiting the scalability of operational crop mapping frameworks.

Beyond hardware constraints, a fundamental limitation of many current
state-of-the-art sequence models is their "temporal myopia".
These architectures typically treat each growing season as an entirely isolated event,
attempting to classify crops based on just one year of weather and growth patterns.
In doing so, they completely ignore a foundational rule of agronomic management: crop rotation.
Farmers intentionally alternate specific crop species across consecutive years to maintain soil health,
prevent nutrient depletion, and break pest cycles.
If a satellite observation is heavily obscured by clouds or spectrally ambiguous in a given year,
a single-year model lacking inter-year context has no historical background to help it classify the vegetation.
Conversely, a model capable of tracking multi-year rotational dependencies can analyze what the farmer
planted in the previous and subsequent years to probabilistically infer the missing crop.

This thesis evaluates a machine learning framework designed to navigate hardware limitations while
simultaneously capturing both intra-year phenology and inter-year crop rotation dynamics.
To overcome the severe HPC bottlenecks associated with large-scale spatial processing,
the framework transitions the data pipeline to a highly optimized 1D parcel-aggregated extraction method.
By utilizing a 10-meter inward erosion buffer to eliminate mixed boundary pixels,
and reducing the data representation to the deep temporal evolution of the spatial mean of
individual agricultural fields, the architecture completely bypasses network I/O limits.
This dimensional reduction enables the rapid processing of a massive six-year continuous SITS
dataset derived from the Austrian INVEKOS (Integrated Administration and Control System) registry,
encompassing approximately 300,000 crop fields across 47 distinct agricultural classes.
To support rigorous architectural evaluation, the project utilizes an automated Machine
Learning Operations (MLOps) pipeline integrating PyTorch Lightning, Ray Tune, and Optuna
to facilitate fair, distributed hyperparameter tuning.

The research systematically establishes robust single-year baselines to serve as standard
benchmarks for phenological extraction.
These include a 1-Dimensional Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN) and standalone Transformer
Encoders evaluated in both generative and discriminative variants.
Additionally, to handle irregular temporal gaps specifically within the Transformer models,
the framework implements an established AgroDays Positional Encoding approach.
Drawing on state-of-the-art techniques in remote sensing, this method adapts standard sinusoidal
encodings using the exact agricultural day of the satellite acquisition to explicitly inject temporal
distance into the spectral embeddings.
Crucially, the research also introduces linear structural controls; in these tests,
the deep non-linear feature extractors are replaced by a zero-memory linear backbone that
temporally mean-pools the sequence and feeds directly into multi-year sequence heads.
The resulting performance degradation in these specific configurations highlights the challenges of
sequence modeling when both non-linear feature extraction and intra-year temporal information are not employed.

To bridge the gap between intra-year phenological tracking and historical agricultural dependencies,
two distinct multi-year hybrid architectures are compared.
The established Transformer-HMM cascade acts as the Bayesian reference model.
In this framework, a generative Transformer acts exclusively as the emission model,
generating unbiased yearly class likelihoods, while a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) models the temporal
sequence using explicit probabilistic transition matrices empirically initialized from the dataset.
Compared with this, the thesis proposes a novel Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM,
a purely neural end-to-end architecture.
This proposed model processes intra-year sequences in parallel using self-attention,
extracting semantic yearly predictions, and feeds this chronological sequence into an inter-year
Bidirectional LSTM.
By confronting explicit probabilistic transition modeling against deep recurrent memory,
this thesis evaluates whether a network can intrinsically learn the latent,
non-linear representations of multi-year crop rotations without relying on explicitly
defined Markovian state transitions.

Experimental results across the 47 distinct crop classes demonstrate the severe limitations
of single-year models when confronted with highly imbalanced agricultural datasets.
The 1D-CNN and standalone Discriminative Transformer achieved limited Macro
F1-scores of 30.17\% and 33.18\%, respectively, frequently defaulting to majority staple
crops at the expense of minority classes.
The linear structural controls suffered significant performance degradation,
with the Linear-LSTM configuration yielding a Macro F1-score of merely 1.04\%.
Ultimately, both multi-year architectures, evaluated across the full six-year sequence,
substantially outperformed the single-year baselines.
This demonstrates that historical crop rotation context is highly effective at rescuing
minority classes that traditional models ignore.
The established Generative Transformer-HMM achieved a robust Macro F1-score of 63.95\%.
The novel Discriminative Transformer-LSTM successfully learned multi-year agricultural cycles end-to-end,
reaching an optimal Macro F1-score of 68.47\% and an Overall Accuracy of 82.07\%,
marking a clear improvement over both the single-year counterparts and the HMM cascade.

This performance suggests that deep recurrent memory cells can intrinsically capture complex
temporal dependencies to improve accuracy, surpassing empirically initialized statistical matrices.
However, experiments also revealed that the Generative variant of the LSTM achieved a significantly lower Macro F1-score (40.12\%). 
This suggests that while unconstrained recurrent networks can learn multi-year transition rules end-to-end,
their performance in this setup remains highly susceptible to the specific probabilistic formulation of their input features,
identifying a clear need for future interpretability studies.
By evaluating this optimized software framework, this thesis provides a computationally
feasible solution for operational longitudinal crop mapping.
The findings lay the groundwork for future research, which aims to improve scalability
and context integration through the exploration of linear-time State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba,
the reintroduction of spatial structures via Graph Neural Networks (GNNs),
and the fusion of multimodal, weather-independent Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data.
