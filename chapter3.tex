\chapter{Experiments, Datasets, and Infrastructure}
\label{cha:experiments}


\section{Experimental Setup and Datasets}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

To rigorously evaluate the proposed multi-year architectures against the established single-year baselines,
a standardized experimental framework was designed. All models were trained, validated,
and tested using identical data splits to ensure a strictly fair comparative analysis.

\subsection{The INVEKOS Dataset and Sentinel-2}
\label{subsec:invekos_sentinel2}

To evaluate the proposed sequence-modeling architectures, experiments were conducted using data
derived from the Austrian \textbf{INVEKOS} (Integrated Administration and Control System) registry \cite{invekos_austria}.
INVEKOS serves as the European Union's official spatial registry for agricultural parcel identification.
It is important to clarify that this dataset provides administrative reference data based on farmers' declarations,
which inherently contains a degree of declarative noise and administrative boundary shifts,
rather than representing a flawless, pixel-perfect ground truth.
To focus the analysis, the dataset extracts data specifically from the Military Grid Reference System (MGRS) tile 33UVP,
chosen for its consistent orbital coverage and highly diverse crop distribution \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.

The input features are constructed from Earth Observation data provided by the European Space Agency's \textbf{Sentinel-2} mission \cite{drusch_sentinel2_2012}.
The study utilizes 805 Level-1C (L1C) Top-of-Atmosphere acquisitions \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
While the 13 native multi-spectral bands possess varying spatial resolutions (10m, 20m, and 60m),
the lower-resolution channels were spatially resampled to a consistent 10m resolution using a nearest neighbour approach \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
In this process, the larger 20m and 60m pixels are subdivided to fit the 10m spatial grid,
and each new 10m pixel is simply assigned the exact spectral reflectance value of its closest original parent pixel.
This specific technique is utilized because it perfectly preserves the original, raw radiometric data
without mathematically interpolating or inventing new intermediate values that could distort a crop's true spectral signature.
To convert the raw digital numbers into a standardized Top-of-Atmosphere (TOA) reflectance scale,
the pixel values were mathematically divided by the sensor's absolute quantification value (typically 10,000) extracted directly from the Sentinel-2 metadata.
This ensures the neural networks process normalized, physically meaningful radiometric signals.
No cloud screening was applied, maintaining the raw sequence with its natural atmospheric occlusions \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.

To facilitate multi-year sequence modeling, the dataset was preprocessed to isolate a strict temporal sequence across six consecutive agronomic years.
An agronomic year in this context is defined as the continuous observation period from September 1st of the preceding year to September 1st of the harvest year.
Consequently, the temporal sequence of satellite images acquired between September 1, 2016, and September 1, 2017, is strictly associated with the final 2017 harvest label.
To construct the final dataset, strict filters were applied: fields were required to have a minimum size of 400 $\text{m}^2$ \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
Crucially, an inward erosion buffer of 10 meters was applied to all parcel
geometries before extracting the spectral signatures.
Because Sentinel-2's highest spatial resolution is 10m per pixel,
this erosion guarantees that only pure, unmixed pixels strictly residing
within the field's interior are aggregated.
Parcels whose valid geometric area fell below the 400 $\text{m}^2$ threshold after this buffering
process were strictly excluded, naturally dropping fields that became effectively empty.

Furthermore, the dataset was filtered to retain only fields that maintained a consistent geometric presence across all six target years \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
In agricultural administrative registries, parcels are frequently merged or subdivided by farmers between seasons;
selecting only stable geometries ensures that the multi-year sequence models are analyzing
the exact same plot of land over time without artificial spectral shifts caused by changing borders.
Additionally, the retained fields were filtered to include only those whose declared crop types
possessed a dataset occurrence greater than 0.05\% \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
While this strict temporal footprint deliberately excludes intermittent fields, shifting borders,
and extremely rare crops, it is mathematically necessary to establish a stable population for sequence evaluation.
After filtering, the dataset encompasses \textbf{47 distinct crop classes} across approximately \textbf{300,000 crop fields} \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.

Crucially, rather than processing memory-intensive 2D spatial patches, each resulting crop field is collapsed into a
single 1D entry representing the \textbf{spatial mean value} of the pixels belonging to that specific field on every acquisition date.
Therefore, the unit of analysis and classification for all models in this study is the individual field.
The data extraction and pre-processing pipeline utilized in this thesis was originally designed and implemented by Perantoni et al.,
allowing this research to bypass standard preprocessing bottlenecks and focus entirely on architectural development.

To rigorously evaluate the architectures without data leakage, the approximately 300,000 filtered
fields were partitioned into Training (60\%), Validation (20\%), and Test (20\%) splits.
Rather than utilizing a naive random split, a stratified random sampling approach was employed based on the
uniqueness of the multi-year label sequences.
An agglomerative hierarchical clustering algorithm (utilizing average linkage) was
applied to group agricultural fields that exhibited similar crop rotation patterns \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
To account for identical crop rotations that are simply out of phase (e.g., a "Wheat-Maize-Soy" rotation started in 2016 versus 2017),
a rotating Hamming distance metric was utilized, building upon the foundational sequence alignment logic established by Hamming \cite{hamming1950error}.
This metric calculates the minimum distance across all possible circular shifts of the multi-year time series,
effectively recognizing temporally shifted sequences as the same core agricultural strategy.
The 60/20/20 split was subsequently executed across the resulting clusters.
This prevents data leakage by ensuring the Test set evaluates the models on strictly unseen geographic parcels, 
while maintaining a proportionally representative distribution of the multi-year rotation patterns seen during training. 
This setup focuses the evaluation on the models' ability to generalize learned rotational rules to new geographical parcels and varying atmospheric noise, 
rather than testing their ability to predict entirely novel, unseen crop rotation strategies.

Training deep sequence models on massive, high-dimensional satellite time series is computationally demanding.
To facilitate this, all experiments were executed on a dedicated High-Performance Computing (HPC) node equipped with an AMD Ryzen 7 5800X processor,
64GB of system RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 GPU, running on an Ubuntu 22.04 LTS environment.

A primary challenge in processing the INVEKOS dataset is the severe input/output (I/O) bottleneck caused by loading thousands of multi-year temporal sequences.
Initial exploratory experiments attempted to utilize the full 2D spatial patches of the agricultural parcels.
Because the massive dataset could not be stored on the local solid-state drive (SSD) and had to be hosted on a shared network folder,
the immense volume of network-based disk reads caused severe GPU starvation.
This rendered the 2D training process computationally infeasible within the available time constraints of this research.
Consequently, this exploratory phase was aborted before a formal comparative benchmark could be established;
exact performance metrics (such as peak VRAM, specific loading times, or precise per-epoch durations) for the 2D pipeline were not rigorously recorded. 

To bypass this severe observed limitation, the experimental framework was forced to pivot to an optimized \textbf{1D pixel-based data pipeline}
provided by Perantoni et al. \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.
Rather than processing broad spatial context, each sample is shaped as a 1D sequence containing the 13 spectral
bands measured across the specific agricultural days of the year.
This aggressive dimensionality reduction completely bypasses the network I/O limits,
rendering the multi-year SITS training executable on a single computing node.
Therefore, the utilization of the 1D pipeline is presented here not as a benchmarked computational contribution of this work,
but rather as a strict engineering prerequisite to make the architectural comparisons possible.


\subsection{Data Loading Strategies}
\label{subsec:data_loading}

To properly evaluate the impact of multi-year memory, the experimental framework utilized two distinct data loading
strategies tailored to the architectural needs of the models. 

For the single-year baseline models (1D-CNN and standalone Transformers), the dataset was structured using an \textbf{intra-year loader}.
This loader decoupled the temporal sequence of the dataset, extracting and serving only a single growing season at a time.
This constrained the baselines to rely entirely on localized, intra-year phenological signatures for classification.

For the multi-year hybrid architectures (Transformer-HMM and Transformer-LSTM), a \textbf{multi-year loader} was employed.
This loader extracts continuous six-year SITS windows for each field,
maintaining the strict chronological sequence across multiple growing seasons.
This structured temporal context is what feeds the inter-year sequence models,
allowing them to learn sequential agricultural practices.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:evaluation_metrics}

Agricultural land cover datasets are inherently unbalanced; a few dominant crop types often account for the vast majority of cultivated land,
while specialized crops represent a tiny fraction of the dataset.
Consequently, relying solely on overall accuracy can yield highly misleading evaluations.
To provide a comprehensive assessment, the models were evaluated using two primary metrics: \textbf{Overall Accuracy} and the \textbf{Macro F1-Score}.

\textbf{Overall Accuracy (OA)} measures the total proportion of correctly classified agricultural parcels across all available classes. It is defined as:
$$ \text{OA} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{C} TP_i}{N} $$
where $C$ is the total number of target classes (47 in this study), $TP_i$ represents the True Positives for class $i$, and $N$ is the total number of samples.

\textbf{Macro F1-Score} is the primary metric used to evaluate true model performance. The F1-score for a single class is the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall.
To compute the Macro F1-score, the F1-score is calculated independently for each of the 47 classes and then averaged with equal weight across classes:
$$ \text{Macro F1} = \frac{1}{C} \sum_{i=1}^{C} \left( 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision}_i \times \text{Recall}_i}{\text{Precision}_i + \text{Recall}_i} \right) $$
Unlike a weighted average, the Macro F1-score treats every class equally, regardless of its support size.
A model can only achieve a high Macro F1-score if it successfully identifies both the dominant and the minority crop classes,
making it a rigorous benchmark for this highly imbalanced dataset.
To guarantee a strictly fair comparative analysis and validate the aggregate model behaviors,
the Overall Accuracy and Macro F1-score are calculated using the exact same mathematical procedure,
across the exact same field-year samples from the test set, and strictly over the identical 47 classes
for all evaluated models (baselines, Transformer-LSTM, and Transformer-HMM).


\section{Infrastructure and MLOps}
\label{sec:infrastructure_mlops}

The complexity of simultaneously training intra-year feature extractors and inter-year sequence heads required
a robust software infrastructure to manage the workflow efficiently on the available hardware.

\subsection{Training Framework and Optimization}
The entire experimental codebase was developed in Python utilizing the \textbf{PyTorch} deep learning framework,
wrapped within \textbf{PyTorch Lightning} \cite{falcon2019pytorch}.
PyTorch Lightning was selected to streamline the development process and ensure code reproducibility.
While the framework natively supports distributed multi-node and multi-GPU training,
all experiments in this thesis were executed concurrently as independent trials on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 GPU.

The networks were trained using the \textbf{AdamW optimizer} \cite{loshchilov2017decoupled},
which explicitly decouples weight decay from the gradient updates to improve generalization.
A crucial optimization strategy for the Transformer-HMM was the implementation of decoupled learning rates.
By assigning independent learning rates to the neural backbone and the sequence head, the automated tuner could independently scale the weight updates,
ensuring the neural network learned effectively without destabilizing the HMM's probabilistic transition matrices.
All models were optimized against a Categorical Cross-Entropy (CCE) loss function.

\subsection{Automated Hyperparameter Tuning}
To ensure all baseline and hybrid models were compared fairly, an automated Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) pipeline was utilized.
\textbf{Ray Tune} \cite{liaw2018tune}, integrated with the \textbf{Optuna} search algorithm \cite{akiba2019optuna},
was deployed to conduct hyperparameter sweeps across concurrent trials on the same machine.
This combination utilized Bayesian optimization to intelligently search the parameter space (specifically learning rates),
aggressively pruning unpromising configurations.
Furthermore, all model executions, configurations, and resulting performance metrics were systematically tracked and
versioned using a centralized \textbf{MLflow} server \cite{zaharia2018accelerating}.

To ensure rigorous and reproducible optimization, all hybrid architectures were tuned using an automated Ray Tune and Optuna MLOps pipeline.
During the tuning phase, models were evaluated across $30$ total trial configurations ($\text{tune\_epochs} = 5$)
to search over hyperparameters such as learning rates, weight decays, and LSTM dimensions depending on the model selected.
The optimal configuration for each model was selected based on maximizing the validation Macro F1-score (\texttt{sval\_f1\_te}) on the validation set. 
For final reporting, the selected optimal configurations were trained for $40$ epochs ($\text{final\_epochs} = 40$) using a
batch size of $32$ and optimized with the AdamW optimizer.
To account for weight initialization variance and ensure statistical robustness, all final test metrics reported in this study represent the mean and standard
deviation computed across $3$ independent training runs ($N=3$ using random seeds $42$, $43$, and $44$) using the optimal configurations.

To provide a concise overview of the selection process and the distinct configurations utilized across the model variants,
a synthesis of the hyperparameters is presented in Table \ref{tab:tuning_summary}.
Furthermore, the tuning search space was strictly tailored to the sequence head: HMM configurations decoupled the learning
rate of the backbone from the transition matrix to ensure stability, while the LSTM configurations explored varying depths and hidden dimensions.

\subsection{Temporal 1D-CNN vs. Transformer Encoders}

The \textbf{1D-CNN} established a strong baseline by effectively mapping local spectral variations.
However, as shown in Table \ref{tab:baseline_comparison}, it achieved a moderate overall accuracy of 55.30\% but
struggled significantly with class imbalance, recording a mean Macro F1-score of only 30.17\%.
The model's sliding receptive field of five observations likely struggled to bridge the temporal gaps caused by prolonged winter cloud cover.

Under the evaluated protocol, the discriminative Transformer achieved a mean Macro F1
of 33.18\%, compared with 30.17\% for the 1D-CNN.
However, it is necessary to declare a structural limitation in this comparison: the Transformer models received
explicit temporal information (the acquisition dates) via the positional encoding, whereas the 1D-CNN received no
explicit time information and relied entirely on the implicit order of the irregular sequence.
Consequently, this baseline experiment evaluates two systems with different information baselines,
isolating the combined effect of self-attention and temporal encoding rather than self-attention alone.

Furthermore, the explicit comparison between the two Transformer formulations yielded critical insights.
While the \textbf{Discriminative Variant} achieved a marginally higher overall accuracy (55.57\% vs 53.47\%),
the standalone \textbf{Generative Variant} yielded superior performance on the minority classes,
achieving a higher Macro F1-score (36.01\% vs 33.18\%).
By removing the bias terms and enforcing a strict, normalized probability space,
the Generative Transformer evaluates the pure spectral evidence without defaulting to dataset priors. 
Furthermore, the application of a temperature scalar ($\tau = 0.1$) acts as a critical
hyperparameter to calibrate the entropy of the output distribution.
Rather than producing perfectly uniform noise or collapsing into completely
one-hot vectors (which would destroy and bypass the HMM's mathematical smoothing capabilities),
this temperature calibration balances the emission likelihoods.
It ensures they provide soft, meaningful probabilistic evidence to the downstream transition matrices \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}.

\subsection{Linear Structural Controls}
To evaluate the multi-year sequence heads without the aid of deep neural backbones,
they were deliberately paired with a \textbf{zero-memory linear backbone}.
In these control configurations, the raw 1D SITS data was processed through a single linear projection before being passed to either the HMM or the LSTM.

The evaluated Linear-HMM configuration obtained a Macro F1 of 5.23\%.
This result motivates investigating the role of the feature extractor,
but does not establish a general requirement for deep nonlinear backbones.
The \textbf{Linear-HMM} configuration managed to maintain a baseline level of predictive power (5.23\% F1-score and 41.52\% Accuracy).
Because the HMM relies on an explicit, empirically optimized transition matrix, this agronomic "safety net" allowed it to partially smooth over the highly overlapping,
low-confidence emission probabilities generated by the linear layer. 

In contrast, the \textbf{Linear-LSTM} configuration struggled significantly, yielding a Macro F1-score of merely 1.04\% and an Overall Accuracy of 32.41\%.
These results document the severely limited performance of this specific configuration on the dataset.
However, because this baseline employs temporal mean-pooling, it simultaneously removes both
non-linear feature extraction and intra-year chronological information.
Consequently, while the performance drop is evident, it cannot be exclusively attributed to the linear projection alone,
as the complete loss of temporal dynamics likely contributes to this degradation.

\section{Multi-Year Architecture Results}
\label{sec:hybrid_results}

The final phase confronted the established \textbf{Transformer-HMM} Bayesian cascade against the novel \textbf{Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM}.
For the multi-year architectures, the Generative Transformer-HMM is systematically compared against both the generative
and discriminative variants of the Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM.
Both architectures vastly outperformed the single-year baselines, definitively proving that analyzing multi-year historical crop
rotations provides a massive predictive advantage.

The \textbf{Transformer-HMM} provided excellent temporal smoothing,
correcting isolated intra-year misclassifications by referencing its explicitly defined, empirically optimized transition matrices. 
To rigorously evaluate the architecture, the Generative Transformer-HMM was evaluated
using three independent training runs with different random seeds on the same fixed train/validation/test split. 
Specifically, the dataset partitioning remained strictly identical across all runs to prevent evaluation variance,
while the different random seeds solely dictated three stochastic components: the initial neural network weight distributions,
the random dropout masks applied during training, and the shuffling sequence of the training dataloader.
This methodology ensures the final averaged metrics are statistically robust against initialization
randomicity, while evaluating exactly the same geographical parcels.
As established in recent literature \cite{perantoni_bayesian_2024}, the generative variant provides the soft,
unbiased emission probabilities mathematically required by the HMM to properly function,
allowing the Generative Transformer-HMM to act as the robust probabilistic baseline with a Macro F1 of 63.95\%.

In direct contrast, the novel \textbf{Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM} demonstrated superior capabilities.
The \textbf{Discriminative} variant yielded the optimal configuration, reaching an impressive Macro F1-score
of 68.47\% and an Overall Accuracy of 82.07\%, definitively outperforming the HMM cascade. 
This result fundamentally reverses initial assumptions.
Unlike the HMM, which explicitly requires normalized probability distributions,
the LSTM thrives when fed the explicitly biased posterior probabilities of the discriminative backbone.
We hypothesize that the deep recurrent memory of the LSTM can effectively leverage these sharper class signals to learn complex,
non-linear multi-year transitions end-to-end, surpassing the empirically optimized transition matrices of the HMM in this configuration.

Conversely, the \textbf{Generative} Transformer-LSTM struggled to maintain this performance,
achieving a Macro F1 of 40.12\% and an Overall Accuracy of 74.52\%.
Relying strictly on aggregate metrics, this performance drop indicates a
distinct behavioral limitation compared to the discriminative variant.
We hypothesize that without the bias terms in the feature extractor,
the LSTM's gating mechanisms might struggle to process the unbiased prototypical space effectively.
However, this internal mechanism remains an unproven hypothesis requiring future interpretability studies.


\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experimental results validate the central premise of this research: relying solely on intra-year phenology severely
limits crop classification performance, particularly on highly imbalanced datasets.

\subsection{Single-Year vs. Multi-Year Capabilities}
\label{subsec:single_vs_multi_year}

The improvement from the single-year Transformers to the multi-year hybrids highlights a fundamental limitation
of traditional models: they evaluate each growing season in a vacuum.
In agricultural reality, planting decisions are heavily governed by strict multi-year crop rotation practices designed to prevent soil depletion.
By feeding a sequence of yearly embeddings into the inter-year sequence heads (LSTM or HMM), the network learns these transitional probabilities.
If the network is uncertain about a crop's spectral signature in year $t$, it can mathematically infer the likely crop by analyzing the confident
predictions from years $t-1$ and $t+1$. 
The bidirectional models perform retrospective classification using observations from the full six-year sequence.

\subsection{The Impact of Class Imbalance}
\label{subsec:class_imbalance}

The INVEKOS dataset suffers from severe long-tail class imbalance, dominated by staple crops. This imbalance is fully exposed by the baseline models.
The 1D-CNN achieved an overall accuracy of 55.30\%, but an F1-score of only 30.17\%.
This discrepancy indicates a network exhibiting high prediction frequencies for majority classes and significant error rates on minority crops \cite{johnson2019survey}.

The hybrid multi-year models significantly mitigate this failure mode.
This is demonstrated by the \textbf{Discriminative Transformer-LSTM}, which achieved a Macro F1-score of 68.47\%,
outperforming the empirically optimized transition matrices of the HMM (63.95\%) in these specific configurations.
We hypothesize that when provided with sharp, biased confidence logits, unconstrained recurrent memory might more effectively
learn to navigate extreme class imbalance end-to-end to rescue minority classes.

However, the susceptibility of recurrent networks to the input formulation is exposed by the lower performance of the \textbf{Generative} variant (40.12\% F1).
Relying on aggregate metrics, we hypothesize that the LSTM might require the dataset priors
explicitly embedded within standard biased logits to properly regulate its transitions.
While empirical transition matrices (like those in the HMM) are inherently robust to unbiased generative inputs,
unconstrained recurrent networks appear to require discriminative signals to navigate extreme imbalance in this setup.
Nevertheless, these explanations regarding the gating mechanisms remain hypotheses,
and targeted interpretability studies are required to definitively confirm this internal behavior.

\subsection{Computational Efficiency}
\label{subsec:comp_efficiency}

Despite its architectural complexity, the Hierarchical Transformer-LSTM proved to be highly computationally efficient during training.
This efficiency is attributed to the model's hierarchical design.
Self-attention mechanisms allow for significantly more parallelization than processing deep temporal time steps sequentially through a recurrent network.
By leveraging the Intra-Year Transformer to evaluate the dense intra-year sequences concurrently,
the sequential computation burden on the inter-year LSTM is reduced to a sequence length of merely six steps (representing the six years of data).
This demonstrates that fusing intra-year attention with inter-year recurrence is computationally feasible on
standard workstation hardware, effectively bypassing the severe I/O bottlenecks associated with full 2D
spatial patches, rather than proving absolute large-scale scalability.
